Saturn has a six-sided storm at its north pole.

This is not a metaphor. NASA has the photographs. It was first spotted in 1981, when Voyager 1 flew past and sent back grainy images that nobody could quite explain.

A hexagonal cloud pattern. Perfectly geometric. Sitting at the top of the planet like a crown.

The Symbol

A hexagon is the shadow of a cube.

Once you start looking, the black cube is everywhere.

Mainstream scholarship does not connect these. The Kaaba, tefillin, and Roman Saturn worship developed independently across radically different cultures and centuries. The "Saturn cube" synthesis is mostly a 20th and 21st century construction. But the cubes? The cubes are real.

December, 1945

The jar in the desert.

In December of 1945, an Egyptian farmer found a sealed jar buried at the base of a cliff. He thought it might contain a demon. He hoped it might contain gold. He smashed it open with a pickaxe.

The setting was the Jabal al-Tarif, a limestone cliff about eleven kilometres north-east of a town called Nag Hammadi in Upper Egypt. The cliff face is honeycombed with ancient tombs from the Sixth Dynasty, four and a half thousand years old.

Two brothers from the al-Samman clan, Muhammad and Khalifah, were digging at the base with their camels tethered nearby. They were not archaeologists. They were farmers looking for sabakh, a nitrogen-rich soft soil they used as fertiliser. Their mattocks hit something hard.

A reddish earthenware jar. Almost a metre tall. Sealed with a bowl-shaped lid coated in bitumen.

Muhammad hesitated. He believed a jinn might be sealed inside. He stood there with his pickaxe raised, weighing the fear of releasing a demon against the possibility of gold.

Gold won.

He smashed the jar. Particles of papyrus flew out into the desert air.

Inside were thirteen leather-bound books. Codices. Roughly fifteen hundred years old. They were not gold. They were something far more disruptive.

They were texts the Roman church had spent four centuries trying to erase from history.

The way these books survived was that someone, in the fourth century, refused to burn them.

By 367 CE, Athanasius, the Bishop of Alexandria, had sent out an Easter letter listing the twenty-seven books to be considered the official New Testament. Every other Christian text was to be rejected as apocryphal. Forbidden. Heretical. To be destroyed.

The Pachomian monasteries of Upper Egypt were directly affected. A leading scholarly theory is that monks at one of these monasteries took the texts they could not bring themselves to burn, sealed them in a jar, and buried them at the foot of the cliff.

They sat there for 1,578 years.

Until a farmer with a pickaxe and a need for fertiliser swung in the right place.
