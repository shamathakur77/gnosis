Yaldabaoth.
The blind god.

Shama Thakur · Sovereign by Source
scroll ↓
A warning before we start

This part is darker than the others.

The Gnostic texts on Yaldabaoth are not gentle. They call the creator of the material world a fool. They call him blind. They call him jealous, ignorant, sadistic, and a liar. If the god you grew up with is the god of Genesis, this essay will name him in ways that will land hard.

Read it slowly. Or close the tab. Both are fine. The Gnostics did not invent this critique to score points. They lived inside the Roman empire, watching the institution of religion become a tool of control, and they wrote down what they thought they were actually looking at.

Their answer is uncomfortable. Take what is true for you. Leave the rest.

Three names, one being

He has three names in the text. None of them flatter him.

The Apocryphon of John names him three times, in three registers. Each name says something the others cannot.

Yaldabaoth. The most likely etymology is the Aramaic yalda bahut, child of chaos. Or possibly child of Sabaoth. It is the name his mother knew him by, the structural fact of his origin.

Saklas. Aramaic for fool. This is what he is, in himself.

Samael. From a Hebrew root meaning blind. Or god of the blind. This is the name he earns when he speaks the line that gives him away.

The line

"I am God, and there is no other."

In the Apocryphon of John, Yaldabaoth wakes up inside the cloud Sophia placed him in. He looks around. He sees the seven offspring he has produced (we will name them in Part 4), and the angels, and the firmaments he has built. He sees no one above him. He sees nothing past the cloud.

So he speaks.

"I am God, and there is no other God beside me."

This line is exact. It is a direct, deliberate echo of two passages in the Hebrew Bible.

Isaiah 45:5: "I am the LORD, and there is no other; apart from me there is no God."

Isaiah 46:9: "I am God, and there is no other; I am God, and there is none like me."

The Gnostic author is not being subtle. They are putting one of the most absolute self-declarations in the Hebrew Bible into the mouth of an ignorant lower being who genuinely does not know there is anyone above him. The implication is doctrinal earthquake. The god who said this in Isaiah is not the highest god. He is a smaller being who has not yet seen the rest of the cosmos.

This is the moment Sophia, watching from the ninth heaven, realises the scale of what she has set in motion. She moves and the heavens tremble. Yaldabaoth hears her and he says it again. He keeps saying it. He cannot un-say it.

From this moment on, the text calls him Samael. The blind one. Because what he said proved he could not see what was above him.

What he made

A copy of a copy of a copy.

The Gnostic critique of the material world is not that it is evil. The critique is more precise than that. The critique is that it is imitative. Yaldabaoth, having stolen some of his mother's light, builds a cosmos in imitation of what he half-remembers from the Pleroma. He cannot build the original because he has never seen it directly. He builds a model of a model.

The text says he creates seven heavens, with a firmament for each. He populates each with rulers. He invents a calendar (365 angels, one for each day). He calls himself God of these heavens. None of his creatures know there is anything above the seventh.

And then he comes to the question of humans.

This is the moment in Genesis where God says, let us make man in our image. The Gnostic reading is that the plural is real. Yaldabaoth says it not to the Trinity but to his fellow archons. They co-create Adam between them, each contributing one feature. Goodness creates the bone-soul. Forethought creates the sinew-soul. And so on through the seven powers.

But Adam does not move. He lies on the ground like a corpse. None of the archons can animate him. Their imitation lacks the spark.

So Sophia, watching, sends a luminous thought through the archons themselves. The text uses different mechanisms in different versions, but the result is the same. Yaldabaoth, deceived by the higher powers, blows the spirit of life into Adam. He thinks he is giving Adam something of himself. What he is actually doing is transferring the last of his stolen light, the light he took from his mother, into the human being.

Adam stands up. He is now luminous. The archons see the light in him and they realise, too late, what has happened. The light they have been trying to contain is now inside their creature.

And they realise something else. The creature is now smarter than they are.

The inversion

This is the part that breaks people.

In the Gnostic reading, the serpent in the Garden of Eden is not the villain. Yaldabaoth is. The fruit of the tree of knowledge is exactly what its name says it is. Knowledge. Gnosis. The capacity to know who you actually are and where you actually came from.

When Yaldabaoth tells Adam and Eve not to eat from the tree, he is not protecting them. He is keeping the prison locked. When the serpent says "your eyes shall be opened", the serpent is telling the truth.

The Hypostasis of the Archons (NHC II,4) calls this directly. The female spiritual principle enters the serpent and uses it as a teacher. Eve receives the wisdom and shares it with Adam. The archons panic. They expel the humans from the garden, not because the humans have done something wrong, but because the humans now know too much.

The Gnostic Eve is not the cause of the fall. She is the cause of the awakening.

This is why the texts were buried

You can see, reading this, why a fourth century bishop building a state religion would find these books intolerable. They do not say the church is wrong on a detail. They say the entire orientation is upside down. The god being worshipped is the jailer. The fruit being warned against is the key. The woman being blamed is the awakener.

This is not the kind of disagreement you can fold into your canon. It has to be erased.

Athanasius's letter went out in 367 CE. Within a generation, most known Gnostic texts in the empire were gone. The ones that survived survived because somebody, somewhere in the Pachomian monasteries of Upper Egypt, sealed thirteen codices in a jar and buried them at the foot of a cliff.

The texts spent more time underground than they had spent above it.

Reading this in 2026

You do not need to believe any of this literally.

This is the place to be careful. The Gnostic cosmology can read, to a modern person, like a coherent description of life inside any system that asks you to deny your own knowing. Religion. Patriarchy. Empire. Algorithm. Anything that says "I am the only god, there is no other, do not eat from the tree."

That resonance is real. It is also a metaphor, and the texts know it. The Apocryphon of John presents itself as a revelation from the risen Christ. The Hypostasis of the Archons is openly mythological. None of these texts demand that you take Yaldabaoth as a literal alien overlord.

What they ask is harder. They ask you to consider that the voice in your head that says "I am the only authority, do not look beyond me, do not eat from the tree" may not be the voice of the highest part of you.

It may be the voice of something much smaller, that simply cannot see anything above itself, and has confused its own ceiling with the actual sky.

Sophia, your reading companion
This one is heavy. Ask anything.

Sophia answers questions on Yaldabaoth, the demiurge concept, and the Gnostic inversion of Genesis. She will not preach. She will not push you toward or away from the god you grew up with.

A question to sit with

The Gnostic critique is not that the god of the material world is evil. It is that he cannot see past himself, and confuses his own ceiling with the actual sky.

Where in your life is something doing that to you right now?
